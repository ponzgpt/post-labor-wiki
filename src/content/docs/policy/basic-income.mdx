## What the evidence shows

<Tag kind="empirical" />Across seven randomised trials of government cash transfers in six developing countries, researchers found no systematic evidence that cash discourages work <Cite id="banerjee2017lazy" at="abstract" />. In the US, a review finds that most unconditional programmes have no significant effect on whether people work, and where there is an effect it is small: a 10% rise in income cuts labour supply by about 1% <Cite id="marinescu2018" at="abstract" />. The same review links cash to better health and education and to less crime and substance use, especially among the most disadvantaged young people.

The pattern is consistent: people keep working, a few work somewhat less, and most report more security. The largest recent US trial found the biggest change in time use was leisure, not training or job search <Cite id="vivalt2024" at="abstract" />.

## What it has not shown

None of these is a national UBI. All gave cash to a minority while jobs, taxes and prices stayed as they were, so they cannot tell us how a whole economy responds when everyone is paid, or how a UBI would be financed.

## Where post-labor thinkers stand

<Tag kind="normative" />David Shapiro treats universal programmes as the base tier of household income, but argues cash alone leaves people dependent on whoever pays it; ownership and power have to come with it <Cite id="shapiro2025six" at="part V" />.

<Skeptics>
- **Cost and targeting.** A UBI would shift transfers towards childless, non-elderly and middle-income households, and one large enough to raise the incomes of poor families would be enormously expensive <Cite id="hoynes2019" at="abstract" />.
- **Pilots cannot settle it.** The same review argues that the ongoing pilots will do little to resolve the main open questions, because they are small, temporary and partial <Cite id="hoynes2019" at="abstract" />.
- **Work does fall when payments are generous.** In the three-year US trial, participation fell 4.2 points and partners worked less too <Cite id="vivalt2024" at="abstract" />. In a post-labor world that may be the point; in today's economy it has a fiscal cost.
</Skeptics>
