## The proposal

<Tag kind="proposal" />Universal basic services (UBS) extend the logic of free healthcare and schooling to other essentials. A 2017 report for UCL's Institute for Global Prosperity costed four additions for the UK at about 2.3% of GDP, and showed they could be paid for by cutting the income-tax personal allowance to £4,300 a year <Cite id="ucl2017" at="p. 13" />. It valued the services at £126 a week for someone who used them all, with the largest gains for the lowest-paid <Cite id="ucl2017" at="p. 13" />.

The same report contrasts this with basic income: a UK payment at the jobseeker's allowance rate for every citizen would cost around 13% of GDP <Cite id="ucl2017" at="p. 21" />.

## What the evidence shows

<Tag kind="evidence" />Services already redistribute a lot. Across OECD countries, counting the value of public education, health, social housing and care reduces measured income inequality by roughly a fifth to a third, depending on the measure <Cite id="verbist2012" at="abstract" />.

There is no trial of a full UBS package. The evidence is on single services, delivered in specific places.

## Where post-labor thinkers stand

<Tag kind="proposal" />Shapiro's base tier, "the universals", explicitly includes services as well as cash and capital endowments <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="plate 3" />, and his appendix adjusts its public-wealth measure to count asset-funded services alongside cash <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="appendix, 'Count the benefit once'" />.

<Skeptics>
- **No cash, no choice.** Services meet needs the provider defines; they do not give households money to spend as they choose or a stake in the economy, the core of economic agency.
- **No leverage.** A population that depends on state-run services has no more bargaining power than one that depends on state-paid cash, the problem Post-Labor Economics puts at its centre <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'The Central Reframe'" />.
- **Quality and rationing.** Free services are rationed by queues and budgets rather than prices; the costing assumes services of adequate quality can be provided at scale.
- **Funding still comes from wages.** The UCL costing pays for UBS from income tax; if wage income shrinks, that base shrinks with it.
</Skeptics>
