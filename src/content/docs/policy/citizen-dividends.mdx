## Alaska: a universal, permanent dividend

<Tag kind="empirical" />Since {nominal[0].x}, every eligible Alaskan has received an annual share of the Permanent Fund's oil-financed earnings <Cite id="akdor-pfd" at="summary table" />. In {cpiYear} prices it has averaged {usd(avg)} a year, ranging from {usd(low.v)} in {low.x} to {usd(peak(realPfd).v)} in {peak(realPfd).x}; the {last(nominal).x} payment was {usd(last(nominal).v)}.

It is the best natural experiment on a permanent, universal cash payment. Comparing Alaska with similar states, economists find it had no effect on employment and raised part-time work by 1.8 percentage points, probably because spending the dividend creates local jobs <Cite id="jones2022" at="abstract" />.

## Norway: a fund without a dividend

<Tag kind="empirical" />Norway's Government Pension Fund Global was worth NOK 19,742 billion at the end of 2024 <Cite id="nbim2024" at="section 1, table 1" />. It pays no dividend to citizens. Instead the government may spend, on average, the fund's expected real return, estimated at about 3% a year, which covers almost a fifth of the state budget <Cite id="nbim-about" at="'About the fund'" />.

## Where post-labor thinkers stand

<Tag kind="normative" />Sovereign wealth funds paying automation dividends are central to Labor/Zero; Shapiro cites Norway, Alaska and Singapore as precedents <Cite id="shapiro2025movement" at="launch post" />. In his pyramid, public wealth is the second tier, above universal programmes <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="plate 3" />.

<Skeptics>
- **Too small to live on.** Even at its peak, Alaska's dividend was a supplement, not an income; a fund large enough to replace wages would have to own a large share of the economy.
- **Volatile and political.** The chart shows how much the payment swings from one year to the next; a household cannot plan on it.
- **Funds need a windfall.** Both funds were built from oil revenue. An automation fund would need a comparable source, such as taxes or equity stakes in AI firms, which is exactly what is contested.
- **Norway shows a trade-off.** Paying returns through the budget funds services for everyone, but gives citizens no direct, portable claim of the kind Post-Labor Economics wants.
</Skeptics>
