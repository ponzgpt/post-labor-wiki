## The idea

<Tag kind="proposal" />Post-Labor Economics wants most household income to come from owning, not working, and names the routes: public wealth funds, employee ownership, birthright capital grants and public equity stakes in AI and robotics firms, especially those built on public infrastructure and data <Cite id="shapiro2025movement" at="launch post" />. These sit in the middle tiers of his Pyramid of Prosperity <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="plate 3" />.

## The evidence, instrument by instrument

- <Tag kind="evidence" />**Employee ownership** has the deepest record. Owned firms show more stability, higher survival and fewer layoffs in recessions; US public firms with employee ownership were about 20% more likely to survive over 12 years than matched firms <Cite id="kruse2022" at="pros; evidence discussion" />.
- <Tag kind="proposal" />**Baby bonds** were designed to narrow the racial wealth gap by endowing children in poorer families more generously, using wealth rather than race as the criterion <Cite id="hamilton2010" at="abstract" />. Connecticut's programme is the first large one; its accounts cannot be claimed until 2041, so outcomes are years away <Cite id="ct-babybonds" at="overview" />.
- <Tag kind="proposal" />**Data dividends** remain a proposal. Their advocates argue that a data labour movement, competition or regulation could rebalance power against data monopsonists <Cite id="arrieta2018" at="abstract" />.
- <Tag kind="proposal" />**Public equity stakes in AI** have no direct precedent; wealth funds are the nearest model (see [citizen dividends](/policy/citizen-dividends/)).

<Skeptics>
- **Concentrated risk.** Workers who hold much of their wealth in their employer's stock can lose job and savings together; portfolio analysis suggests keeping such holdings to a modest share <Cite id="kruse2022" at="cons; evidence discussion" />.
- **Free-riding.** Rewards shared across a firm can weaken individual incentives, and results depend on employee involvement, job security and training <Cite id="kruse2022" at="cons" />.
- **Scale.** A $3,200 endowment grows into a useful start in life, not an income. Replacing wages would need ownership stakes far larger than any programme has tried.
- **Who pays for data?** If a few firms capture data's value, pricing it requires bargaining power the individuals whose data it is do not have, which is why its advocates call for countervailing power <Cite id="arrieta2018" at="abstract" />.
</Skeptics>
