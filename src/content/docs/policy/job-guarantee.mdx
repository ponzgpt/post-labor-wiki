## The idea

<Tag kind="normative" />Instead of paying people not to work, the state offers work to anyone who wants it, at a set wage. The offer sets a floor under wages and conditions, and keeps the social side of a job: routine, status, colleagues, purpose.

## What the evidence shows

<Tag kind="empirical" />In India, the employment guarantee pushed up wages and employment in the private labour market as well, which the authors read as evidence of employers' market power: workers' reservation wages rose and landowners' returns fell <Cite id="muralidharan2023" at="abstract" />.

In Austria, a guaranteed-job pilot in the village where a classic 1930s study of unemployment was set found strong gains for participants, including social status, time structure and a sense of collective purpose, and a fall in long-term unemployment across the municipality. It found no effect on physical health <Cite id="kasy2026" at="abstract" />.

## Where post-labor thinkers stand

<Tag kind="analytical" />A job guarantee is the opposite bet to Post-Labor Economics. It assumes the problem is a shortage of jobs that the state can fill; PLE assumes paid work itself is becoming optional and wants to end *obligatory* labour <Cite id="shapiro2025movement" at="launch post" />. The Austrian evidence on meaning and status is still relevant: it shows what a post-labor society would need to replace.

<Skeptics>
- **Costly per person.** The Austrian pilot cost about €237 per participant per month while raising participants' income by about €102 <Cite id="kasy2026" at="abstract" />; the rest pays for organising the work.
- **Useful work is hard to create.** Guaranteed jobs must be real enough to give purpose without displacing paid workers; the Austrian study found no displacement in one small municipality, which is not a national test.
- **It ties income to work.** If machines do most tasks better and cheaper, a guarantee pays people to do work for its own sake, which post-labor thinkers argue is laborism by another name <Cite id="shapiro2025movement" at="launch post" />.
</Skeptics>
