This wiki covers a contested subject, so it has to be checkable. Every factual sentence links to its source, and every source sits in one [bibliography](/bibliography/) with a DOI or a stable link.

## Three kinds of claim

| Label | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| <Tag kind="evidence" /> | Measured: official data, a randomised trial, a peer-reviewed study | Alaska's dividend did not reduce employment |
| <Tag kind="forecast" /> | A model or scenario about the future, however careful | AI will add at most 0.66% to productivity over ten years |
| <Tag kind="proposal" /> | What someone argues we *should* do | Pay every citizen an automation dividend |

Forecasts and proposals can be good arguments. They just aren't facts yet, so they never share a label with evidence.

## Which sources count

1. **Official data**: statistical agencies, central banks, Our World in Data.
2. **Peer-reviewed research** and preregistered trials.
3. **Working papers** from established series (NBER and similar), labelled as such.
4. **Books and essays**, cited for ideas and arguments, not for numbers they don't source.
5. **Commentary**, including David Shapiro's Substack, cited for what its author argues.

AI-generated research reports, including the deep-research PDFs in Shapiro's public repository, are used only to find primary sources; they are never cited.

## Images

Every image is public domain, CC0, CC BY or drawn by us, and the [credits](/credits/) page lists each one's source and licence. Memes are credited like any other source.

## Corrections

Found an error or a better source? [Open an issue](https://github.com/ponzgpt/post-labor-wiki/issues) or use "Edit page" at the bottom of any page. Corrections are made in the open, in the repository's history.

## How this site is made

The research, structure and editorial rules are Javier Ponz's; AI coding agents write the code and help draft text, and every claim is checked against the source it cites before it ships. Writing and original graphics are CC BY 4.0.
