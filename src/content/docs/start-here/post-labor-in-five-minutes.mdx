<Aside type="tip" title="TL;DR">
Machines have always replaced tasks. Post-labor thinkers argue that AI and robotics could replace enough of them that wages stop being how most households get by, so income, and power, have to come from ownership instead. The data show labour losing ground in some measures; whether it is about to fall off a cliff is contested.
</Aside>

## 1. An old dream, a new deadline

In 1930 Keynes warned of "technological unemployment" and predicted that his grandchildren would need only "a fifteen-hour week" <Cite id="keynes1930" at="parts I–II" />. In 1983 Wassily Leontief warned that human labour could go the way of the draft horse after the tractor <Cite id="leontief1983" at="pp. 403–410" />. See the [timeline](/timeline/).

## 2. What the data already show

- <Tag kind="evidence" />The global labour share of income has declined significantly since the early 1980s <Cite id="karabarbounis2014" at="abstract" />.
- <Tag kind="evidence" />In the US, output per hour has grown much faster than typical workers' pay since the 1970s <Cite id="bivens2015" at="figure A" />.
- <Tag kind="evidence" />US local labour markets more exposed to industrial robots lost employment and wages <Cite id="acemoglu2020" at="abstract" />.
- <Tag kind="evidence" />Workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed US occupations are employed about 19% below the path of less-exposed peers, with no economy-wide displacement <Cite id="brynjolfsson2025canaries" at="abstract" />.

More in [the evidence](/evidence/).

## 3. The Post-Labor Economics answer

David Shapiro's Post-Labor Economics starts from what he calls the Great Decoupling: output stops running through wage employment <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="plates 1–2" />. If that holds, the question is not how to save jobs but how households get an income, and keep a say, without them.

<Tag kind="proposal" />His answer is to make ownership, not wages, the main way income is distributed: sovereign wealth funds paying automation dividends, wider employee ownership, universal basic capital grants and public stakes in AI built on public data <Cite id="shapiro2025movement" at="launch post" />. He calls basic income necessary but not sufficient: prosperity measures need power measures beside them <Cite id="shapiro2025six" at="part V" />. See [the framework](/frameworks/post-labor-economics/).

## 4. What real experiments say

<Tag kind="evidence" />Cash without conditions has been tested for decades:

| Programme | What people got | Headline result |
|---|---|---|
| Mincome, Manitoba, 1974–79 | A guaranteed annual income | Hospitalisations 8.5% lower than controls <Cite id="forget2011" at="abstract" /> |
| Alaska, since 1982 | A yearly dividend for every resident | No effect on employment; part-time work up 1.8 points <Cite id="jones2022" at="abstract" /> |
| Finland, 2017–18 | A monthly basic income for 2,000 unemployed people | Employment statistically unchanged in year one <Cite id="verho2022" at="abstract" /> |
| Kenya, GiveDirectly | About $1,000 per poor household, once | Local multiplier of 2.5, minimal inflation <Cite id="egger2022" at="abstract" /> |
| Texas and Illinois, 2020–23 | $1,000 a month for three years | Earned income down about $1,900 a year; leisure up most <Cite id="vivalt2024" at="abstract" /> |

None of these tested a world without jobs. They show how people respond to cash while jobs still exist. More in the [policy toolbox](/policy/).

## 5. The strongest doubts

- <Tag kind="evidence" />Automation complements labour as well as replacing it <Cite id="autor2015" at="abstract" />, and most US jobs today are in specialties that did not exist in 1940 <Cite id="autor2024newwork" at="abstract" />.
- <Tag kind="evidence" />The most precise study so far finds no effect of generative AI on earnings or hours two years after ChatGPT <Cite id="humlum2025" at="abstract" />.
- <Tag kind="forecast" />A task-based estimate puts AI's ten-year boost to total factor productivity at no more than 0.66% <Cite id="acemoglu2024" at="abstract" />. Post-labor advocates reply that such models miss machines becoming better, faster, cheaper and safer at nearly everything <Cite id="shapiro2026seven" at="intuition 1" />.
