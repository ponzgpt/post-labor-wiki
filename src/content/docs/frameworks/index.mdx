<div class="matrix-wrap">

| Framework | Diagnosis | Where income comes from | What happens to work |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Post-Labor Economics** (Shapiro) <Cite id="shapiro2025six" at="part V" /> | Machines will out-compete most labour; power and income must be rebuilt around ownership | A floor of universal programmes, then public wealth, collective and private assets | Optional; a residual "meaning economy" |
| **Basic income** (Van Parijs and Vanderborght) <Cite id="vanparijs2017" at="ch. 1" /> | Freedom requires a material floor for all | An unconditional cash payment | Chosen, with more bargaining power |
| **Universal basic services** (UCL IGP) <Cite id="ucl2017" at="report summary" /> | Essentials are cheaper to provide collectively than to buy with cash | Free public services, cash second | Continues; costs of living fall |
| **Post-work left** (Srnicek and Williams) <Cite id="srnicek2015" at="ch. 6" /> | Full automation is desirable if its gains are shared | Basic income plus a shorter working week | Shrinks by design |
| **Technofeudalism** (Varoufakis) <Cite id="varoufakis2023" at="chs. 3–4" /> | Platform owners already extract rents from everyone else | Rents flow to owners of cloud capital | Persists, under worse terms |
| **Steer the technology** (Acemoglu and Johnson) <Cite id="acemoglu2023" at="prologue" /> | Who gains from technology is a political choice | Wages, if innovation is pushed towards augmenting workers | Continues, made more productive |
| **Still so many jobs** (Autor) <Cite id="autor2015" at="abstract" /> | Automation complements as well as replaces labour | Mostly wages | Changes form, as before |

</div>

The first five expect paid work to matter less and disagree about who should own what replaces it. The last two doubt the premise, or think it can be changed. This wiki describes each in its proponents' terms and gives each a skeptics' box.
