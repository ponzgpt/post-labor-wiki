## The argument

<Tag kind="analytical" />Shapiro's central claim here is that the biggest risk of automation is not lost income but lost bargaining power. Ordinary people won concessions because elites needed their work; if work becomes optional, people may keep an income but lose any way to influence those who pay it <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'The Central Reframe'" />. He traces that leverage to strikes, desertion and the threat of revolt in the industrial era <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'The Historical Mechanism'" />, and calls the mutual need between states and citizens "double bilateral dependence" <Cite id="shapiro2026seven" at="intuition 4" />.

That is why he calls basic income necessary but not sufficient: prosperity measures need power measures beside them <Cite id="shapiro2025six" at="part V" />. The second pyramid, built from the bottom up, is his list of what would give citizens new leverage <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="plate 4" />.

## The default path

<Tag kind="forecast" />Without new institutions, Shapiro expects competition between states and firms, plus automation, to drift towards technofeudalism: high technology with low quality of life for most <Cite id="shapiro2026technofeudalism" at="parts II–IV" />. He sees the way out in cooperation at ever larger scales and in new channels of household income <Cite id="shapiro2026technofeudalism" at="parts IX–X" />. Yanis Varoufakis argues a version of this has already happened, with platform owners collecting rents rather than profits <Cite id="varoufakis2023" at="chs. 3–4" />.

## The proposed tools

| Lever | Examples he discusses | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Wealth funds, citizen dividends, capital grants, employee ownership | Precedents exist: [toolbox](/policy/) |
| Transparency and identity | Open records, digital identity, open payment rails | National examples cited; untested as leverage <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'Alternative Leverage Mechanisms'" /> |
| Participation | Quadratic voting, participatory budgets, citizens' assemblies | He proposes testing them at scale |
| Legal and physical | Private rights of action, whistleblower bounties, zoning and energy approvals | Depend on courts and democracy staying intact <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'Alternative Leverage Mechanisms'" /> |

He also urges using the leverage labour still has to lock these institutions in before it fades <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'The Time Dimension'" />.

<Skeptics>
- **Power does not only come from labour.** Votes, courts, parties and public opinion constrain elites in societies where most people are children, retired or otherwise outside paid work. Shapiro himself notes that unions' legal gains outlasted their leverage <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'The Historical Mechanism'" />.
- **The digital tools are unproven as leverage.** He concedes that transparency and deliberation platforms work only when tied to real enforcement or decision power <Cite id="shapiro2026democracy" at="'Alternative Leverage Mechanisms'" />.
- **Technology can be steered.** Acemoglu and Johnson argue that whether machines replace or augment workers is a political choice, so policy could push innovation towards augmentation instead of accepting full automation <Cite id="acemoglu2023" at="prologue" />.
- **Default-path claims are hard to falsify.** "Technofeudalism by default" is a forecast; it needs indicators that would show it is wrong, not only signs that it is right.
</Skeptics>
