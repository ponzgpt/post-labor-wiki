## The idea

<Tag kind="proposal" />Economic agency is a household's ability to secure its own living. Post-Labor Economics tracks it by splitting income into three buckets, wages, transfers and capital, and argues that as wages fall the other two must rise, with capital the most neglected <Cite id="shapiro2026seven" at="intuition 3" />. The Pyramid of Prosperity orders the institutions that would deliver that income, broadest first <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="plate 3" />.

## Two ratios to measure it

<cite>Labor Zero</cite>'s free appendix defines two measures <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="appendix, 'How the ratios fit together'" />:

- **Capital Allocation Ratio (CAR):** recurring ownership income divided by wages plus transfers. A CAR of 0.25 means capital supplies one fifth of that income; a CAR of 4, four fifths.
- **Public Wealth Ratio (PWR):** the part of household resources that comes through qualifying shared institutions, so that broad ownership can be told apart from ownership by a few.

The appendix also sets tests for what counts as inclusive capital, including broad eligibility, real yielding assets and benefits independent of employment <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="appendix, 'Count the benefit once'" />.

The shift so far is mostly from wages to transfers, not to ownership: the opposite of the path PLE wants. And a national share says nothing about how many households receive that capital income.

<Skeptics>
- **Ownership income is already highly unequal**, so raising capital's share without changing who owns it could widen gaps rather than close them <Cite id="piketty2014" at="introduction" />.
- **Returns are not guaranteed.** Capital income depends on returns that fluctuate; Alaska's dividend has swung widely from year to year (see [citizen dividends](/policy/citizen-dividends/)).
- **The arithmetic is demanding.** Replacing most wage income with dividends would take broadly held assets far larger than any public fund today; the appendix itself works through how much capital an income floor needs and lists the evidence that could challenge the framework <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="appendix, introduction" />.
- **A ratio is not a mechanism.** CAR adds no new information beyond capital's income share; its value is in directing attention <Cite id="shapiro2026laborzero" at="appendix, 'How the ratios fit together'" />, so it must be paired with policies that actually move assets.
</Skeptics>
