## Leaving the farm

<Tag kind="empirical" />Around 1400, most of the workforce in England and France worked the land ({f1(uk[0].v)}% and {f1(fr[0].v)}%). By {last(uk).x} the shares were {f1(last(uk).v)}% and {f1(last(fr).v)}%. The world is still on the way: {f1(world[0].v)}% in {world[0].x}, {f1(last(world).v)}% in {last(world).x}. The biggest labour-saving shift in history did not leave people idle; the work moved elsewhere.

## The first decoupling: Engels' pause

<Tag kind="empirical" />The transition was not painless. In Britain, output per worker rose 46% between 1780 and 1840 while the real wage rose only 12%; profits took a growing share of income until wages finally caught up later in the century <Cite id="allen2009" at="introduction" />. Labour's share can fall for two generations even when work does not disappear.

## Telephone operators

<Tag kind="empirical" />Between 1920 and 1940, AT&T replaced operators with mechanical switching across over half its network. The jobs vanished, but later cohorts of young women were not less employed: clerical and service work, including new kinds of jobs, absorbed them. Incumbent operators bore the cost, and a decade on were more likely to be in lower-paid jobs or out of work <Cite id="feigenbaum2024" at="abstract" />.

## Fewer hours, not fewer jobs

<Tag kind="empirical" />Productivity was also taken as time. Hours per worker in the US fell from {h0(hUS[0].v)} a year in {hUS[0].x} to {h0(last(hUS).v)} in {last(hUS).x}; in Germany from {h0(hDE[0].v)} to {h0(last(hDE).v)}.

<Skeptics title="What the analogues do and do not show">
Every transition so far created new tasks and sectors, and automation complemented as much work as it replaced <Cite id="autor2015" at="abstract" /> <Cite id="acemoglu2019" at="abstract" />. Manufacturing jobs also fell for reasons beyond automation, including trade. <Tag kind="analytical" />Post-Labor Economics replies that people sell only four things, strength, dexterity, cognition and empathy, and that machines are now gaining on all four at once <Cite id="shapiro2026seven" at="intuition 2" />.
</Skeptics>
