Post-labor arguments rest on a claim about the present: that human labour is already losing ground. This section tests it against measured data only. If machines were taking over paid work, the indicators below would move first: labour's slice of income down, ownership and transfers up, fewer prime-age adults in work, fewer hours.

<Aside type="tip" title="In short">
The labour-share and productivity tiles point toward a decoupling of output from labour. The employment tiles point the other way: so far, people are still working. Both are true, and the pages below show why.
</Aside>

Each tile shows the latest annual value, the change over its full history and a sparkline; follow its title for the full chart and the debate. <Tag kind="analytical" />The "what to watch" lines are our reading of what each indicator would show if the post-labor thesis holds; they are not measurements. Every chart reads a committed snapshot of FRED, the ILO, the OECD, Our World in Data or the Alaska Department of Revenue, refreshed with `npm run data`; the date on each tile is when its file was downloaded. The capital and transfer tiles use national accounts, which include imputed items, so they are a rough proxy for household cash income, not Shapiro's own measures.
