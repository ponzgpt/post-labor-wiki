<Aside type="tip" title="TL;DR">
Among Americans aged 25 to 54, men's participation has fallen for seventy years while women's rose steeply until the 1990s and has edged up since. Overall, prime-age participation is near its record. Labour has not disappeared; it has been redistributed.
</Aside>

## Reading it carefully

- Participation is not employment, and it is not hours. A part-time worker counts the same as a full-time one.
- A chart cannot say *why*. The decline among men has many proposed causes; this page makes no claim about which dominates.
- <Tag kind="evidence" />Where cash was given without conditions, people kept working but worked somewhat less: in a three-year US trial, participation fell 4.2 points <Cite id="vivalt2024" at="abstract" />, while Alaska's universal dividend left employment unchanged <Cite id="jones2022" at="abstract" />. See [Post-labor in five minutes](/start-here/post-labor-in-five-minutes/).

<Aside type="caution" title="What skeptics say">
On this measure there is no sign yet of work disappearing: prime-age participation is close to its all-time high. Post-labor advocates answer that the shift they expect is ahead, driven by AI agents and robots, not visible in the past <Cite id="shapiro2025six" />.
</Aside>
