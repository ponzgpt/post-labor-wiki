<Aside type="tip" title="TL;DR">
The biggest labour-saving shift in history already happened: farming went from most people's job to almost nobody's. Factories are repeating the pattern. Both times, the work went elsewhere. Post-labor thinkers ask what happens when there is no elsewhere.
</Aside>

## Why it matters for post-labor

This is the Great Decoupling in miniature: production keeps rising while the human labour it needs falls <Cite id="shapiro2025six" />. Robots played a measurable part in the factory story: US areas more exposed to industrial robots saw employment and wages fall <Cite id="acemoglu2017wp" at="abstract" />.

<Aside type="caution" title="What skeptics say">
Farm and factory workers did not become permanently idle: the economy created new tasks and whole new sectors, and automation complemented as much work as it replaced <Cite id="autor2015" /> <Cite id="acemoglu2019" />. Manufacturing jobs also fell for reasons beyond automation, including trade. The open question is whether AI, which targets cognitive work, leaves anywhere similar to go.
</Aside>
