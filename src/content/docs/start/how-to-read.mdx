This wiki covers a contested subject, so it has to be checkable. Every factual sentence links to its source, and every source sits in one [bibliography](/reference/bibliography/) with a DOI or a stable link.

## Four kinds of claim

<ClaimLegend cards />

Every page declares one primary type in its header. When a page also contains claims of another type, the header lists them and each one carries its badge where it appears, so a measured fact never sits unmarked beside an interpretation. Badges never rely on colour alone: each has a word and an icon, forecasts are dashed and value choices double-ruled. Charts carry the same badge in their frame, and drawings of an idea with round numbers are marked <Tag kind="illustrative" /> so they are not read as data.

<h2 id="framing">Whose framing</h2>

Each page header also says whose framing it follows.

| Chip | Pages | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Follows Shapiro's framing | Post-Labor Economics, economic agency, power after labour | Describes David Shapiro's framework in his terms and structure, with attribution. Description, not endorsement; each page still ends with the strongest case against. |
| Independent synthesis | The evidence, policy options, open questions, frameworks compared, reference | Our own reading of primary data and peer-reviewed work. Where Post-Labor Economics has a position, it appears in a marked, attributed paragraph. |
| PLE framing + independent evidence | Start here | Shapiro's question organises the overview; the evidence beside it is independent. |

The five questions that structure the wiki are ours, not Shapiro's. His own syllabus has six steps; we put the evidence before any framework, his included.

## Which sources count

1. **Official data**: statistical agencies, central banks, Our World in Data.
2. **Peer-reviewed research** and preregistered trials.
3. **Working papers** from established series (NBER and similar), labelled as such.
4. **Books and essays**, cited for ideas and arguments, not for numbers they don't source.
5. **Commentary**, including David Shapiro's Substack and videos, cited for what their author argues.

AI-generated research reports, including the deep-research PDFs in Shapiro's public repository, are used only to find primary sources; they are never cited. His *Labor Zero* companion is © all rights reserved, so this wiki links to it and paraphrases it; it never reproduces it.

## Checked by machine

`npm run check` refuses to build the site unless:

- every `<Cite>` names a source in the registry and gives a locator (page, table, section or "abstract"), and every cited source has passed `npm run verify` or carries a dated note that a person checked it;
- every page declares its claim type and framing, uses only the inline badges it declares, and declares only badges it uses;
- no page has more than 600 words of prose, every framework and policy page has a skeptics box, and no quotation runs to 15 words or more;
- every chart reads a committed snapshot listed in the data manifest, and no page types chart numbers in by hand;
- every internal link, anchor and redirect resolves, and every section index lists all its pages;
- every text colour meets 4.5:1 contrast in both themes, and no colour token falls in the brown and ochre range.

Chart data are refreshed with `npm run data`; the build itself never touches the network.
